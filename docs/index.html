§04 · The insight

Software middleware can be jailbroken.
Cryptography cannot.

Signet is a mandatory co-signer on every agent transaction. Without its signature the quorum is not met, and the protocol itself refuses to settle. Compliance becomes a property of the ledger, not a policy a language model can be talked out of.

§05 · Architecture

A policy engine that holds one of two required keys. The LLM never touches it.

Two-of-two quorum A jailbroken agent can propose anything — without key_b's signature, the protocol refuses.
Revocation in one transaction Single on-chain SignerListSet updates the quorum. No re-deploys.
§06 · A compliant trade, end to end

Six steps. Each produces an artefact a regulator can query.

01 · PROPOSE
Agent emits a typed intent
Structured decision. Typed JSON tool-call binds intent to policy version.
02 · EVALUATE
Policy engine runs the rules
Notional caps, allowlists, daily velocity, KYT score, sanctions — deterministic, per-intent.
03 · ROUTE
Escalation tier decides
INSTANT co-signs. HUMAN routes to a clear-signing UI. Roadmap: NOTIFY, DELAY tiers.
04 · CO-SIGN
Policy key signs in the enclave
Separate-process co-signer; agent cannot access the key. Roadmap: TEE attestation binds the signature to a known binary.
05 · SETTLE
XRPL enforces the quorum natively
2-of-2 multisigned transaction costs ~30 drops. Protocol, not contract, validates.
06 · ATTEST
Merkle commit lands on-chain
Memo carries reasoningCommit. Full trace persists off-chain for regulator disclosure.
§07 · Why XRPL, and why only now

Four primitives on mainnet. No other L1 has all four.

Native Multisig
Long-live · no contracts
SignerListSet · 32 signers, weighted quorum. rippled C++ rejects below-quorum transactions. No deployment surface, no audit burden.
Credentials
Live · September 2025
On-ledger attestations of KYB / KYC. The signing layer can require them before releasing funds to a counterparty.
Permissioned Domains
Live · February 2026
Protocol-native gating of which accounts can transact in a given domain — MiCA-style whitelisting without application-layer trust.
Sub-cent Fees
Base 10 drops
2-of-2 multisigned transaction ≈ 30 drops. Fractions of a cent — high-frequency agent activity is economically viable.

§13 · What we are honest about

The thesis is strong. The details are not free.

L01
Article 14 still requires a human.
The stop button must exist and a trained, authorised person must be able to press it. Signet makes oversight tractable — it does not replace the human.
L02
The policy engine is a high-value target.
Formal verification, fuzz testing, redundant invariants, and a separate high-threshold human multisig with time-lock for any policy change.
L03
XRPL Hooks and Smart Escrows are not yet live.
On-chain rule expressiveness is constrained. The policy engine therefore lives off-chain in HSM / TEE infrastructure and leverages existing primitives.
